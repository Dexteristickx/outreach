Prayer Made Simple

How to Pray: The ACTS Model

If you don't know what to say, follow this historic four-part pattern:

Adoration: Praising God for Who He Is

Begin by setting your eyes on God’s majesty, patience, and love before mentioning your to-do list. Praise Him because He is holy, faithful, and good.

"Lord Jesus, You are the Creator of the stars and yet You know my name. You are full of mercy and slow to anger. I honor and worship You today."

Confession: Honesty About Where You Stumbled

God already knows everything we did and thought. Confession is simply agreeing with God about our sins so His cleansing grace washes our conscience clean.

"Father, I lost my temper today and spoke harsh words. Please forgive me. Cleanse my heart and help me choose gentleness instead."

Thanksgiving: Naming Your Blessings

Gratitude transforms our perspective. Thank God for specific blessings, such as daily provision, family, and the gift of salvation in Christ.

"Thank You, Lord, for waking me up today, for the roof over my head, and for never giving up on me even when I wandered away."

Supplication: Asking for What You Need

Jesus commanded us to ask for our daily bread. Bring your anxieties, your family's health, your financial stress, and your spiritual growth to Him.

"Jesus, please grant me wisdom for this job interview, comfort my grieving friend, and guard my thoughts from anxiety today. Amen."

Spiritual Community

How to Find a Healthy Local Church

The church is not a museum of saints; it is a hospital for sinners learning to love like Jesus.

Green Flags to Look For:

  • The Bible is opened and taught with clarity and reverent humility.
  • Jesus Christ and His finished work at the Cross are the hero of every sermon.
  • People are genuinely warm, diverse, welcoming to visitors, and patient with questions.
  • Leaders are accountable, transparent, and model Christlike servant leadership.

Red Flags to Avoid:

  • Preachers who demand money in exchange for God's blessings or miracles ("Prosperity Gospel").
  • A culture where leaders cannot be questioned and members are controlled through fear or guilt.
  • Isolationism: claiming their church is the only one in the world that has true truth.
